Post-Listing Underperformance in Cryptocurrency Markets

Post-Listing Underperformance on Binance: A Survivorship-Aware Event Study of 472 USDT Listings

Said Bakhtiev Working paper — August 2026

Abstract

We study the post-listing performance of all 472 Binance spot USDT listings from January 2021 to August 2026 using a point-in-time calendar reconstructed from the exchange’s raw archival infrastructure, explicitly retaining delisted tokens that survivor-biased samples drop. Median returns from the first trading-day close are negative at every horizon: −4.45% (+1d), −11.45% (+7d), −21.62% (+30d); bootstrap confidence intervals exclude zero at all horizons, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests reject equality with zero at p < 10⁻¹² throughout. Cross-sectionally, we find that day-0 intraday volatility (the high–low range), rather than the signed opening pop, is the dominant predictor of subsequent underperformance (t = −5.01). Excluding delisted tokens shifts the +7d median by only +0.35 pp in our panel, but we show why universe-based sampling is far more damaging in general. Finally, we translate these findings into a live sector-momentum system and document which candidate improvements survive honest out-of-sample discipline: a market-breadth gate (≥40% of the universe above its 50-day average) combined with stablecoin-supply growth filtering raises daily-momentum Sharpe from 1.30 to 2.53 in backtest, while popular alternatives (intraday trailing stops, time-of-day tilts) are rejected on evidence.

Keywords: cryptocurrency, listings, event study, survivorship bias, momentum, IPO underperformance JEL: G12, G14, G24

1. Introduction

Buying a token when a major exchange lists it is one of crypto’s most persistent folk strategies. The academic analogue — initial public offering underpricing followed by long-run underperformance — has been documented in equity markets for over three decades (Ritter, 1991), and early crypto studies confirmed elevated announcement-window returns (Ante, 2019; Li et al., 2022).

Yet virtually all accessible evidence suffers from a structural flaw: dead coins disappear from data providers, so any backtest built on today’s universe studies only survivors. On equal-weighted small-cap crypto portfolios this bias inflates returns by up to 62 percentage points annually (Ammann et al., 2022). Listing strategies — equal-weight, small-cap, extreme attrition — sit exactly in the worst zone of this distortion.

This paper makes four contributions:

  1. Data. We reconstruct a point-in-time listing calendar for 3683 Binance spot symbols directly from the exchange’s public S3 archives, retaining delisted pairs, and release it openly together with a 25-feature event dataset.
  2. Evidence. On n=472 USDT listings (2021–2026), median forward returns from the first-day close are negative at every horizon tested, with nonparametric significance far beyond conventional thresholds (Wilcoxon p ≤ 10⁻¹²).
  3. Cross-section. The fade intensity is predicted by day-0 range (volatility), not by the signed opening return itself; naive quartile analyses confound the two.
  4. Application. In a live-simulated daily momentum system over liquid memes, we document which research-derived improvements survive disciplined validation (breadth and stablecoin gates) and which do not (intraday trailing stops, time-of-day tilts), including an explicit multiple-testing accounting.

2. Related literature

IPO underperformance is among the oldest documented anomalies in empirical finance (Ritter, 1991; Loughran & Ritter, 1995). Crypto-specific work begins with Ante (2019), who reports significant announcement-window abnormal returns around Coinbase and Binance listings, later extended by Ante & Meyer (2021). Li et al. (2022) confirm large announcement effects (means of +22–33%) while noting the mean/ median asymmetry that characterizes lottery-type payoffs. Industry panels (The Tie Research, n=1844; CryptoRank, n=559) consistently find that the premium accrues before public availability and decays within weeks.

On data integrity, Ammann et al. (2022) quantify survivorship-induced inflation in crypto at up to 62 pp/year for equal-weight portfolios. To our knowledge, no free public dataset of exchange listings retains delisted tokens; ours does.

3. Data and methodology

3.1 Point-in-time calendar

Binance publishes monthly k-line archives to a public S3 bucket (data.binance.vision), including symbols later delisted. We paginate the index by symbol folder (3,695 folders), restrict to daily bars, and define for each symbol: first_month (listing month), last_month, and delisted = 1 if the history ends more than one month before the retrieval date. Filtering to *USDT spot pairs and excluding leveraged-token families (UP/DOWN/BULL/BEAR) leaves 472 events from 2021-01 to 2026-08.

3.2 Price paths

Daily OHLCV paths come from REST klines for surviving symbols and from stitched archive zips otherwise (handling the platform-wide millisecond→microsecond timestamp migration of 2025). Reference price = first-day close. Forward horizons: +1, +3, +7, +14, +30 trading days.

3.3 Truncation

If a delisted token’s path ends before a horizon, the return is marked to the last available close and flagged (trunc_*). Replacing truncated observations with −100% worsens every estimate; conclusions are conservative either way.

4. Results

4.1 Post-listing drift is severe and highly significant

Bootstrap 95% CIs of medians exclude zero at all horizons. Note the mean/median asymmetry: means are dragged toward zero (and statistically insignificant at +30d) by a small right tail of spectacular winners — precisely the lottery structure that makes “average listing return” marketing misleading.

The drift is stable across subperiods: split-half medians at +7d are −11.39% vs −11.67% (Mann–Whitney p = 0.65).

4.2 What predicts the fade: volatility, not the pop

Univariate quartile analysis suggests a monotone gradient — listings with stronger day-0 pops fade harder (strongest-pop quartile: −20.4% median next week vs −0.9% for weakest). However, cross-sectional regression disentangles the two candidates:

Term Coef t-stat

first-week annualized vol +12.57 15.36

Conditional on day-0 range, the signed pop is mildly positive: the univariate gradient is driven by co-movement between extreme pops and extreme intraday volatility. Practically, day-0 range is the robust red flag (each log-unit of range costs ≈8 pp of the following week).

4.3 Survivorship quantification

Within our archive-based panel, dropping delisted tokens shifts the +7d median by only +0.35 pp — small, because the archive preserves dead-coin paths. The relevant warning applies to samples built from live-universe endpoints: there, every token that died before sampling vanishes entirely. Given median −11.45% at +7d and a 71% share of negative outcomes, a survivor-only redraw of comparable magnitude is plausible and consistent with the +62 pp/year upper bound of Ammann et al. (2022) for equal-weight crypto portfolios.

4.4 From anomaly to strategy: what survives validation

We embedded these findings into a daily momentum system over liquid meme sectors (long top-k by trailing return, gated by BTC trend, position sizes inverse to volatility) and tested 25+ candidate refinements against a fixed baseline (Sharpe 1.30, 2005–2026 backtest window; 2019+ effective for memes):

Candidate change Verdict Evidence
Market breadth gate: trade only if ≥40% of universe above SMA50 accepted Sharpe 1.30→2.06; flat plateau across thresholds 30–45%; stable in both sample halves
Stablecoin supply growth gate (>0.5%/30d) accepted robust across 12 window/threshold combos (Sharpe 1.40–1.50 standalone)
Momentum lookback 14d instead of 20d accepted mid-table improvement, not knife-edge
Fresh-listing exclusion (<21d) accepted (defensive) motivated by Table 1
Intraday trailing stops (2–8%) rejected destroys returns (CAGR 145%→4–92%) via noise-triggered exits and missed V-rebounds
Time-of-day tilt (US session) rejected no effect on memes (US hours +0.05% cumulative vs night +0.09%)
RV-percentile exposure cap withdrawn harmful once breadth gate active

Full stack (14d lookback + breadth + stablecoin gates): Sharpe 2.53, CAGR 187%, max drawdown −52%, split-half Sharpes 2.50/2.83. Given ~25 hypotheses tested, selection inflation is real; we treat the stack as an upper bound pending live tracking, which is ongoing.

4.5 Permutation and robustness checks

A permutation test that shuffles forward returns across all event-horizon cells cannot distinguish the +1d and +3d medians from the pooled market (p = 1.00, 0.81) — early horizons are dominated by the market-wide return distribution — but rejects at p = 0.07 for +7d and at p < 10⁻⁴ for +14d/+30d. The drift is invariant to 2% tail-trimming, winsorization, excluding 2021 (meme mania) and excluding Q4-2024: median fwd_7 stays within [−11.52%, −11.31%] across specifications, Wilcoxon p ≤ 1.4×10⁻¹³ throughout (Appendix R2–R3). Relative to round-trip retail costs of ~16 bps, the median absolute move is an order of magnitude larger; monetizing the negative sign, however, requires perp shorts whose funding drag typically exceeds the drift for most names.

4.6 Cross-venue calendars and lottery features

We additionally reconstruct a point-in-time Bybit spot calendar from the same-style public archive (829 USDT listings since 2022; 49.5% already delisted). Overlap with the Binance panel: 205/472 events (43%) are listed on both venues. Listing order across venues does not predict post-Binance performance (Binance-first −9.7% vs Bybit-first −12.8% median fwd_7; Mann–Whitney p = 0.20) — cross-venue sequencing is not a tradable signal at the daily horizon.

Within the first week, tokens exhibiting extreme single-day up-moves (top MAX tercile, median +23.9% best day) subsequently show better +30d outcomes than low-MAX tokens (−14.0% vs −20.6%; corr(max_daily_7d, fwd_30) = +0.32). Naive lottery-reversal expectations are therefore not supported at the monthly horizon; extreme upside days mark continuation, consistent with attention-driven drift rather than instant exhaustion.

4.7 Can public web crawls reconstruct announcement dates? No.

F3-type claims (“the premium accrues before the public announcement”) require point-in-time announcement timestamps, which Binance does not expose historically. We attempt a reconstruction from the Common Crawl quarterly indexes: all 71 collections covering 2019–2026 were queried for binance.com/en/support/announcement/* URLs (client-side will-list slug filter; ticker candidate = last hyphenated token before the 32-hex page hash; futures/margin/options slugs excluded).

The yield is unusable for event dating. Of 472 panel events, only 38 (8.1%) have any matching ticker candidate, and zero fall inside a ±90-day plausibility window around the panel’s listing date (median first-crawl lag: −545 days). Two structural reasons: (i) announcement slugs carry human-readable tickers only from ~August 2022 — earlier slugs are opaque hex strings, so the 2021–2022 listing wave is unrecoverable by construction; (ii) Common Crawl dates are first-crawl, not publication, dates — stale announcement pages surface in crawls years after publication, so first_seen systematically post-dates the true announcement and cannot bound it. Web-crawl archives therefore cannot support announcement-window event studies for Binance spot listings; the Wayback Machine CDX route (publication-date aware) remains the only public archival option and is pending an unrelated outage of web.archive.org at the time of writing. Pre-announcement drift analysis (claim F3 in our falsification matrix) stays outside this paper’s scope.

4.8 Preregistered confirmatory holdout: Bybit

Before any Bybit price data was examined, we froze three directional hypotheses replicating the Binance findings on a different venue, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction (q = 0.05) over the family and a dual decision rule (adjusted p < 0.05 and predicted direction). The cohort is the point-in-time Bybit USDT calendar restricted to tokens still tradeable — Bybit’s public API serves klines only for listed symbols, so price paths come from REST for survivors only (419 of 829 listings; 415 usable; declared limitation). Feature construction mirrors the Binance pipeline exactly.

H1 (post-listing fade) replicates. Median forward return from day-0 close at +7 trading days is −10.34% (Binance: −11.45%), Wilcoxon p = 5.4×10⁻¹⁴, BH-adjusted 8.1×10⁻¹⁴; only 27.5% of tokens are positive after a week.

H3 (first-week extremes mark continuation) replicates. Corr(max daily return of week 1, fwd_30) = +0.373 (Binance: +0.32), inside the predicted +0.2…+0.4 band; top-MAX tercile median fwd_30 −6.74% vs bottom tercile −17.81% (Mann–Whitney p = 0.0024).

H2 (day-0 volatility predicts the fade) does NOT replicate — the sign flips. On Bybit survivors the log day-0 range coefficient is +4.65 pp (t = +2.69) versus −8.21 pp (t = −5.01) on Binance: wider first-day ranges weakly predict better weekly outcomes once conditioning on survival. We report this as a genuine cross-venue / cross-conditioning heterogeneity, not noise (p_BH = 0.0075); it is hypothesis-generating only. A declared deviation: the preregistered delisting-flag control is dropped as identically zero in the survivor-only design.

H4 (survivorship decomposition, Binance panel): not supported as predicted — but dollar-weighting reveals a larger fact. Excluding delisted tokens shifts the median fwd_7 by only +0.35 pp (bootstrap 95% CI [−1.32, +2.16]; prediction was ≥ +1 pp). The value-weighted sub-test became evaluable after refetching true day-0 USD turnover for all 472 events from the death-inclusive exchange archive (base-unit volume weights are degenerate: five mega-supply tokens held 98% of total weight). Result: the USD-weighted median fwd_7 is −28.72% for the full panel versus −27.77% among survivors — a delisting-attributable wedge of just +0.95 pp. Survivorship does not explain the dollar-versus-token gap; that gap (+17.3 pp) exists because high-turnover listings fade harder regardless of subsequent delisting. Survivorship bias operates through listing selection and tail means rather than the weekly median, while money-weighted first-week experience is ~2.5× worse than the token-counted median.

The gap has structure. In turnover quintiles the fade is monotone: median fwd_7 runs −0.3%, −5.0%, −14.5%, −16.8%, −22.7% from the lowest to the highest day-0 USD-turnover quintile — the lowest-turnout listings show no fade at all in week one. Day-0 turnover correlates with day-0 range (Spearman +0.84) and pop (+0.79), retains incremental predictive power in cross-section (−2.3 pp per log-unit, t = −2.5), persists in every calendar year and survives exclusion of the five largest events. This matches the Miller divergence-of-opinion mechanism documented for IPOs (early-market uncertainty proxies predict underperformance; Amihud 2006; Houge et al. 2001): record turnover marks peak attention, prices set by optimists revert, and dollars systematically buy that peak. Exploratory analysis; details in the companion repository (usd_gap_root_cause.md).

5. Limitations

  • Primary panel is one venue (Binance spot). The Bybit holdout (Section 4.8) covers survivors only — REST klines do not exist for delisted symbols — so the cross-venue replication of H1/H3 carries a survivorship caveat by construction; the death-inclusive decomposition (H4) runs on Binance only.
  • Announcement timestamps could not be reconstructed from public archives (Section 4.7); the study covers the post-open window, not the announcement premium documented elsewhere.
  • The cross-venue sign flip in the range→fade gradient (H2) is unexplained; we flag it as heterogeneity rather than resolve it.
  • No historical order-book depth exists publicly; open-market liquidity is proxied by volume features only.
  • Backtest costs (8 bps round-trip) may understate realized slippage in stressed regimes.
